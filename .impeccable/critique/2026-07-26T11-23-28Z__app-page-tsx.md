## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment**: Not obviously AI slop at a glance — the single-accent-color, monochrome-neutral system is genuinely restrained and DESIGN.md's explicit bans (gradient text, side-stripe borders, numbered markers, hero-metric template) are all respected. The templated tell is the repeated icon-chip + title + body card, used verbatim in MedicalSpecialties (6×), Skills (4×), and AchievementsVolunteering (2×) — three uses of the same generic card shape across one page. `ThreeBackground.tsx` also exists in the repo but is never imported — dead code, and it means PRODUCT.md's claim about an "already in use" Three.js background is stale documentation.

**Deterministic scan**: `detect.mjs` ran clean (exit 2, 1 finding) against components/app — a `border-radius: 3px` on `::-webkit-scrollbar-thumb` (`app/globals.css:136`) outside the DESIGN.md radius scale. This is a false positive: cosmetic, non-content, and the kind of scrollbar styling the detector isn't well-suited to judge.

The browser console overlay (live-server injection) found 20 anti-patterns the CLI scan didn't cover, because they're rendering-time, not static-analysis: 3× **low-contrast** text (white on `#f5f5f7`/`#ffffff`, ratios of 1.0–1.1:1 against a required 3:1/4.5:1) on the Hero headline and the Services/Education banner headings; 1× **all-caps-body** (13px uppercase body text); 4× **clipped-overflow-container**; 8× **icon-tile-stack** (corroborates the repeated-card-template finding above); 1× **single-font** (informational, not a defect — DESIGN.md's single-system-font rule is deliberate); 9× **image-hover-transform** (standard interaction pattern, not a real issue — detector noise).

**Visual overlays**: Overlay injection succeeded and was screenshotted — the hero headline box and the Services/Education banner headings are visibly near-invisible against their light backgrounds in the captured evidence. The low-contrast findings compare text color against the *section background color*, not the photographic image actually behind the text in the hero/banners, which somewhat overstates real-world risk since visual contrast varies by image region — but the underlying CSS provides no guaranteed-contrast fallback, so it's worth fixing regardless of what the current photo happens to look like.

## Overall Impression

The visual system itself is the strongest asset here — restrained, disciplined, and true to the "Wellness Atelier" north star in DESIGN.md. What undermines it isn't taste, it's execution gaps that a visitor will actually hit: text that's functionally invisible in specific spots, a CSS sizing bug that clips banner images on every phone, a credibility-breaking contradiction about where she currently practices, and a primary CTA that promises a booking flow that doesn't exist. None of these are aesthetic failures — they're the kind of thing a careful pass would catch, and exactly why this design reads as "assembled from the design system's defaults" rather than fully finished.

## What's Working

1. **Restrained, disciplined visual system** — single accent color, consistent soft-shadow vocabulary, generous whitespace. This is the reason the site doesn't read as a generic aesthetic-clinic ad, and it's a direct, successful execution of PRODUCT.md's "calm confidence" principle.
2. **Content-driven credibility** — real institutions, a real dated timeline, a real CV download. "Credibility through specificity" is working as intended for the parts of the content that are internally consistent.
3. **The About section's humanizing pull-quote + photo + nickname ("Ploy")** does more trust-building (the Oxytocin moment) than any trust badge could — it's the emotional peak of the page.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Hero headline and banner headings are functionally unreadable at points.** Browser-measured contrast of white text against `#f5f5f7`/`#ffffff` backgrounds comes in at 1.0–1.1:1 against a required 3:1 (large text) / 4.5:1 (body) — one Education banner heading is effectively invisible. This is a rendering-time bug the static scan can't see but the live page shows plainly.
*Why it matters*: the hero headline is the single largest text moment on the page (DESIGN.md's "Display" style) — if it's unreadable against certain image regions, the first five seconds Jordan (first-timer) needs to build trust are wasted.
*Fix*: add a guaranteed-contrast scrim/gradient behind the text (not just decorative — DESIGN.md already uses a scrim on desktop hero; extend the same guarantee to mobile and to the Services/Education banner headings) rather than relying on the photo being dark enough wherever text sits.
*Suggested command*: `/impeccable polish`

**[P1] Services and Education banner images are clipped on every mobile width.** `components/MedicalSpecialties.tsx:90` and `components/Journey.tsx:100` use `aspect-ratio: 16/5` combined with only `min-height: 200px` and no width constraint — below 640px content-width (i.e. every phone), the browser sizes the box off the height floor (`200 × 3.2 = 640px`) regardless of actual parent width, and the overflow is silently swallowed by the surrounding `overflow-hidden` wrapper. Measured directly: at 600px viewport, parent width 552px, box rendered at 640px.
*Why it matters*: banner image + text overlay is laid out and cropped against the wrong box on every mobile visit — invisible as a bug (no scrollbar), but the crop is wrong every time.
*Fix*: constrain with `width: 100%` plus `aspect-ratio`, or swap the `min-height` floor for a `max-width` cap so the aspect-ratio math derives from the actual container width, not a fixed height.
*Suggested command*: `/impeccable adapt`

**[P1] About and Experience contradict each other on her current practice.** `About.tsx:63` states, present tense, that she practices "at Inspire IVF Thailand," while `Experience.tsx:39-52` shows Inspire IVF as a past role (Feb–May 2026) and lists Izee Clinic and NURA Bangkok as her actual current positions (both "Jul 2026 — Present").
*Why it matters*: PRODUCT.md explicitly calls out that the page must be "peer-proof" for colleagues doing a credential check — this is exactly the kind of inconsistency that check is designed to catch, and it undercuts trust for exactly the audience segment it needs to hold up for.
*Fix*: update the About blurb to name her current practices, or make About reference the timeline instead of restating it.
*Suggested command*: `/impeccable clarify`

**[P1] "Book Consultation" promises a booking flow that doesn't exist.** Every instance of the CTA (Nav, Hero desktop + mobile, mobile menu) links to `#contact`, which is phone/email cards, not a booking mechanism.
*Why it matters*: this is the Endorphin/relief moment in the emotional journey — the instant a visitor decides to act. A label that overpromises creates a small letdown at the exact point where friction should be lowest, right before the highest-intent click on the page.
*Fix*: either build a real booking path, or relabel to "Contact for Consultation" so the label matches what actually happens.
*Suggested command*: `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] Primary mobile touch targets are undersized, repeatedly.** Two independent passes measured this from source: the hamburger menu button is 36×36px (`Navigation.tsx:99-104`, `p-2` around a `w-5 h-5` icon); the Hero "Book Consultation" CTA and the "Download Full CV" button are both ~36-38px tall (`Hero.tsx:73`, `Journey.tsx:85`, `py-2.5` around 14px text); the Contact page's `tel:`/`mailto:` links are 21px tall with no vertical padding (`Contact.tsx:119-132`). All fall under the 44×44pt minimum. By contrast, the mobile nav menu's own link rows (59px) and its in-menu CTA (~56px) are correctly sized — the inconsistency is specifically on the primary actions, including the one that matters most (calling the clinic).
*Why it matters*: Casey (distracted, one-handed, thumb-only) is a named persona for this register, and the undersized targets sit on exactly the actions — book, download credentials, call — that convert intent into contact.
*Fix*: raise padding on all four to hit 44px minimum (e.g. hamburger to `p-3`, CTAs to `py-3.5`, contact links get explicit vertical padding).
*Suggested command*: `/impeccable adapt`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (Confused First-Timer)**: The hero establishes visual professionalism fast, but real credential proof requires scrolling to About/Journey — and if Jordan reads both (a plausible path, since both frame themselves as "get to know her"), the Inspire IVF contradiction directly undermines the trust the hero just built. "Book Consultation" also sets a false expectation of an actual booking flow that isn't there.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)**: Hamburger button (36×36px) and the Contact tel/mailto links (21px tall) are the two concrete, measured failures — and the second one is the actual conversion action. The mobile nav's own link rows are correctly thumb-sized by contrast, so this isn't a systemic sizing choice, it's an inconsistency: whoever sized the nav menu didn't apply the same rule to Contact.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User)**: Achievement bullets (`Experience.tsx:195-201`) and the volunteering list (`AchievementsVolunteering.tsx:110-125`) are built from `<div>`s, not `<ul>/<li>` — a screen reader loses list semantics and item count entirely. Dead `href="#"` legal links are focusable no-ops with no signal they're not real. On the positive side: `focus-visible:outline-2` amethyst rings are applied consistently across nearly every interactive element, and muted body text (`#6e6e73` on white, ≈5.08:1) comfortably clears AA at the sizes used.

## Mobile-First Responsive Verdict

Both assessment agents independently hit the same `resize_window` flooring behavior noted in prior sessions — every attempt (390×844, 768×1024, 1440×900) landed between 606-768px regardless of what was requested, confirmed via `window.innerWidth` after each resize. Both independently fell back to the iframe-wrapper technique, and Assessment B additionally confirmed the iframe's rendered size directly via `getBoundingClientRect()`: exactly `{width: 390, height: 844}` and, separately, exactly `{width: 768, height: 1024}` — genuine device-width verification, not a floored approximation.

**Verified at true 390px**: Hero, About, Services/MedicalSpecialties, Journey/Education banner. No horizontal overflow at this width; full-bleed images and cards stayed flush. Hero `<h1>` (`clamp(1.9rem, 9vw, 2.8rem)`) wraps cleanly on two lines with no container overflow.

**Verified at true 768px**: desktop nav (not hamburger) renders correctly, no overflow/clipping; the banner-overflow bug above does *not* manifest at this width since available content width (704px) exceeds the 640px trigger threshold — it's specifically a sub-640px (mobile/narrow-tablet) defect.

**Not visually verified at true mobile width** (reviewed from source or at the floored ~606-616px width only, cross-checked against the fact that Tailwind's first breakpoint is `sm:640px` so 390-639px renders identically): Experience, Skills, AchievementsVolunteering, Contact, footer.

**Additional mobile findings**: total scroll depth to reach Contact is ~17,000px (≈21 viewport-heights) if scrolling manually — mitigated by the Hero's direct `#contact` jump link, so the primary action isn't actually scroll-gated. The mobile hero gradient overlay drops to `from-transparent via-transparent to-black/75` (vs. desktop's protective scrim from the top) — one agent photographed an apparent case of the fixed nav becoming illegible against a bright hero region but couldn't reproduce it cleanly in a same-origin tab, so this is flagged as a robustness risk worth a real-device check, not a confirmed defect. No JS errors were observed during scroll/interaction on either the Three.js hero background or the Framer Motion animations, but no quantified performance/FPS measurement was taken — inconclusive, not a pass.

## Minor Observations

- `prefers-reduced-motion` in `globals.css:226-235` zeroes CSS animation/transition durations, but Framer Motion's `useScroll`/`useTransform`-driven scroll parallax in `Hero.tsx:9-17` and every `whileInView` fade run through Framer's own rAF/inline-style pipeline, which that CSS media query can't reach (`grep -rn "useReducedMotion|MotionConfig" components/` returns nothing). PRODUCT.md explicitly requires reduced-motion alternatives "for all animation" — the single most vestibular-provocative motion on the page currently isn't covered. Worth a `/impeccable harden` pass.
- Dead footer legal links (`Contact.tsx:238-239`, `href="#"` for Privacy Policy / Terms of Service) — focusable no-ops for keyboard/screen-reader users.
- Inconsistent quote-mark handling: `About.tsx:154` uses curly-quote entities, `Journey.tsx:176` uses straight quotes for the same pull-quote device.
- `cursor-default` on cards that still lift on hover (`MedicalSpecialties.tsx:131`, `Skills.tsx:134`, `AchievementsVolunteering.tsx:76`) — affordance mismatch.
- Copy references a variable weekly schedule ("two clinics on different days of the week") without ever stating which days or how to find out — the one detail a patient actually needs to act on.
- `ThreeBackground.tsx` is unused dead code; safe to delete, and PRODUCT.md/DESIGN.md's references to it should be updated to match the shipped page.
